> Captured from the URLs package on 2026-04-07.

- The paper studies how insurer behavior interacts with Medicare Part D subsidies in stand-alone PDP markets.
- Using a structural model of supply and demand, the authors find that consumers buy too few and too socially costly PDP plans, while insurers price near marginal cost.
- They argue that the main welfare driver is the opportunity cost of government spending relative to other Medicare programs.
- The paper concludes that the existing subsidy design performs close to an optimal in-kind subsidy but remains far from a social planner's first-best outcome.
- It also emphasizes that links among regular enrollees, LIS assignment, and MA-PD-based subsidy calculations shape both premiums and welfare.
