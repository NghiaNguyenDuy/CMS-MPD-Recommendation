> Captured from the URLs package on 2026-04-07.

- The paper argues that Medicare Part D creates fragmented insurance for most beneficiaries because prescription drugs and other medical care are often covered by separate insurers.
- It finds that integrated MA-PD plans and fragmented stand-alone drug plans face different incentives when designing formularies.
- Relative to fragmented plans, integrated plans more often design formularies to attract beneficiaries who are profitable under Parts A and B.
- The paper also finds that integrated plans more generously cover drugs that can causally reduce medical spending, which suggests some formulary differences reflect real cross-service efficiencies rather than only selection.
- The authors conclude that the basic design of Medicare Part D supports covert selection through drug-level plan design.
