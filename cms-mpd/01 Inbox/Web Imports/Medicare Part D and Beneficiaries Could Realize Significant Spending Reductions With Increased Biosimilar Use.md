- OIG estimated that biologics cost Part D upward of $12 billion annually.
- It analyzed biosimilar use and spending in Part D from 2015 to 2019 and compared biosimilar uptake with reference products.
- In 2019, reference biologics were prescribed about five times more frequently than biosimilars in Part D.
- OIG estimated that Part D spending on biologics with available biosimilars could have been $84 million lower, or 18 percent, if all biosimilars had been used as frequently as the most used biosimilars.
- Beneficiary out-of-pocket costs for those drugs could also have been $1.8 million lower, or 12 percent.
- OIG found that not all formularies covered available biosimilars and that plans rarely used preferential tier placement or utilization management to encourage biosimilar use.
