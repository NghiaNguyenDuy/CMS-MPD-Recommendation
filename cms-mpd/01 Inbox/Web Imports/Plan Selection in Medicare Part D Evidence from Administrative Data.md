- The paper uses Medicare administrative claims data to study whether beneficiaries optimize their Part D plan choices.
- It reports that fewer than 10 percent of beneficiaries enroll in plans that are ex post optimal with respect to total cost.
- Relative to a static decision rule similar to Medicare's Plan Finder, based on the current year's drugs, the paper estimates average losses of about $300 per year.
- The authors argue that the results are difficult to explain by decision costs alone and suggest that many beneficiaries are not optimizing effectively.
