## A Guide to Enrolling in Medicare Part D

Are you nearing Medicare eligibility? It can be challenging to know how comprehensive your health care coverage needs to be. One thing you may be wondering about is, “Will Medicare cover my prescription medications?”

[Original Medicare](https://www.ncoa.org/article/what-is-original-medicare-parts-a-and-b/) (Parts A and B) does not cover the cost of prescription drugs. Those are covered by [Medicare Part D](https://www.ncoa.org/article/what-is-medicare-part-d/) (prescription drug coverage). Part D coverage can be purchased as a standalone plan, or as part of a Medicare Part C ([Medicare Advantage](https://www.ncoa.org/article/what-is-medicare-advantage/)) plan.

## Do I need to enroll in a Part D plan?

It’s not mandatory to join a Medicare Part D plan. But enrolling in a Part D plan is important if you rely on prescription medications to support your health and well-being—or if it’s likely you will in the future.

“Without Part D coverage, the cost of medications can quickly become overwhelming for most older adults, especially since our health care needs can change as we get older,” said , NCOA Director for Health Coverage and Benefits. “A Part D plan helps protect you from the potentially high costs of current and future prescriptions, so you can get the medications you need at an affordable price.”

Like original Medicare, Part D is not free; there are out-of-pocket costs to consider. For example, you will have to pay a monthly premium—the average estimated Part D premium in 2026 is $34.50. You will also have to pay for your prescriptions up to a certain amount before your plan's coverage kicks in. This is called the deductible (capped at $615). Learn more about [Medicare Part D out-of-pocket costs for 2026](https://www.ncoa.org/article/how-much-does-medicare-part-d-cost/).

## When can I enroll in a Medicare Part D plan?

Just like with Medicare Parts A and B, you can enroll in a Part D plan during your [Initial Enrollment Period](https://www.ncoa.org/article/medicare-enrollment-periods/). This timeframe begins three months before you turn 65, includes your birth month, and ends three months after you turn 65. If you miss this window, you can also enroll during the Open Enrollment Period, which runs from Oct. 15 to Dec. 7 each year (with coverage starting on January 1 of the following year).

Additionally, if you have a qualifying life event, such as moving out of your plan’s service area, losing other creditable prescription drug coverage, or moving into a nursing home, you may be eligible for a [Special Enrollment Period](https://www.ncoa.org/article/what-are-the-medicare-special-enrollment-periods-seps/) (SEP) to join or change your Part D plan.

## How do I enroll in a Medicare Part D plan?

First, take the time to compare different Part D plans using Medicare's online [plan finder tool](https://www.medicare.gov/plan-compare/#/?year=2025&lang=en). Once you've chosen a plan that fits your health needs and budget, you can [enroll](https://www.medicare.gov/drug-coverage-part-d/how-to-get-prescription-drug-coverage):

- Online at [Medicare.gov](https://www.medicare.gov/)
- Directly on the insurance provider's website
- By calling Medicare at 1-800-MEDICARE (1-800-633-4227) or by calling the insurance company directly
- By completing a paper application and mailing it to the insurance company

## What happens if I don’t enroll in a Part D plan during designated enrollment periods?

If you delay enrolling in Part D, you could face a lifetime [late enrollment penalty](https://www.ncoa.org/article/medicare-part-d-late-enrollment-penalty/) when you eventually sign up for a Part D plan. This penalty only applies if you didn't have other creditable drug coverage and you don't qualify for [Medicare Extra Help](https://www.ncoa.org/article/medicare-part-d-how-to-get-extra-help-paying-for-prescriptions/). The penalty is added on to your monthly Part D premium, and it typically lasts for as long as you have drug coverage through Medicare. The amount is based on the length of time you were without prescription drug coverage.

### Can I delay enrollment in Part D and keep my current drug coverage?

Yes, you can delay enrollment in Medicare Part D and keep your current drug coverage if your existing coverage is considered "creditable." This means it’s as good as or better than Medicare's standard prescription drug coverage.

Creditable coverage can include employer-sponsored plans, union health plans, Veterans Affairs (VA) health insurance, or certain other types of health insurance. Check with your current insurance provider to confirm whether your drug coverage is creditable.

### Can I enroll in Part C/Medicare Advantage instead of Part D to get drug coverage?

[Medicare Advantage plans](https://www.ncoa.org/article/what-is-medicare-advantage/) are private health plans that have contracts with Medicare. These plans must cover all the same benefits as Medicare Parts A and B, and most also cover prescription drugs. Enrolling in a Part C plan that covers prescription drugs can replace the need to enroll in a Part D plan.

### What if I need help paying for my prescriptions?

If you have trouble affording your medications, help is available. The Medicare Part D Low Income Subsidy (LIS, or "Extra Help") is a program that helps people with low income save on their prescription drug costs. Social Security and Medicare run this program together.

Extra Help may cover your monthly Part D premium, deductible, and pharmacy copays if you qualify. The estimated average value of this benefit is about $5,700 a year. Learn more about getting Extra Help with your prescriptions.

### I have questions about Medicare enrollment—who can I talk to?

Choosing the right Medicare coverage is a big decision. If you have questions about Part D or other parts of Medicare, it's a good idea to talk to someone knowledgeable about this program before you enroll. Below are two options for getting accurate and unbiased guidance:

- **Contact Medicare directly:** Speak to a customer support representative by calling 1-800-MEDICARE (1-800-633-4227). TTY users can call 1-877-486-2048. You can also [start a live chat](https://www.medicare.gov/talk-to-someone) on the Medicare website. Support is available daily, except during some federal holidays.
- **Contact SHIP:** State Health Insurance Assistance Programs (SHIPs) nationwide offer free, trustworthy counseling to current Medicare beneficiaries and those who are eligible to enroll. [Find your local SHIP office here](https://www.shiphelp.org/) or call 1-877-839-2675.
