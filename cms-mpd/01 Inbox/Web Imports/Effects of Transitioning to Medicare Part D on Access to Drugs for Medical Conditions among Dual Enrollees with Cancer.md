- The study evaluates how the shift from Medicaid drug coverage to Medicare Part D affected use of noncancer treatments among dual enrollees with cancer.
- Using a 5 percent national sample from 2004 to 2007, it examines the removal of Medicaid prescription caps under Part D.
- The study found increased use of lipid-lowering medications after the transition and suggests modest improvements in access overall.
- Among Black beneficiaries in capped states, it found increased use of lipid-lowering therapy and antidepressants and reduced white-Black gaps in several medication-use measures.
