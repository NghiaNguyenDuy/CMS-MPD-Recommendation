# Summary

These KFF materials describe how the Medicare Part D market changed from 2024 through early 2026, including enrollment growth, shrinking stand-alone PDP choice, continuing sponsor concentration, benchmark-plan pressure for LIS beneficiaries, and redesign-era premium and deductible shifts.

## Key Points

- KFF reports that 54.8 million beneficiaries were enrolled in Part D plans in 2025, and CMS enrollment had risen to 56.1 million by February 2026.
- The market remains split between stand-alone PDPs and MA-PDs, but the mix is changing. In 2025, 58% of Part D enrollees were in MA-PDs and 42% were in stand-alone PDPs, while by February 2026 the share was 56% MA-PD and 44% PDP because employer group PDP enrollment rose sharply.
- Stand-alone PDP choice is shrinking. KFF reports 464 PDPs offered in 2025 and 360 PDPs in 2026, with average non-group PDP options falling from 14 in 2025 to 11 in 2026.
- Sponsor concentration remains high. The top 5 firms covered 77% of all Part D enrollees in 2024 and 73.5% in 2025, meaning market structure remains dominated by a small set of parent organizations even as plan menus change.
- Premium experience differs sharply by plan type. In 2025, nearly 80% of MA-PD enrollees without LIS paid no monthly Part D premium, compared with 31% of PDP enrollees without LIS.
- Deductibles became more common in 2025, especially in MA-PDs. KFF reports that 60% of MA-PD enrollees and 85% of PDP enrollees were in plans charging a deductible for drug coverage in 2025.
- LIS benchmark pressure is now a practical enrollment issue. KFF reports 90 premium-free benchmark PDPs in 2025 and 88 in 2026, the lowest counts since Part D began, with 1.1 million LIS PDP enrollees in 2025 exposed to premiums unless they switched or were reassigned.
- The redesign changed market incentives as well as beneficiary cost sharing. KFF's 2026 materials tie premium changes, premium stabilization, and sponsor strategy directly to the new out-of-pocket cap and higher plan liability above the cap.

## Why It Matters

This note gives the vault a market-structure frame for plan comparison. It shows why beneficiaries, counselors, and researchers can see the same policy changes very differently: a formal $2,000 cap may improve affordability while plan menus narrow, benchmark options shift, and premiums move in response to redesign-era liability.

## Useful Evidence

- The 2026 enrollment brief shows that employer group PDP enrollment rose by 1.2 million while employer group MA-PD enrollment fell by 1.2 million, which is an important caution against reading aggregate PDP or MA-PD shares as purely consumer preference.
- The 2025 first-look brief reports that the number of firms sponsoring stand-alone PDPs fell from 11 in 2024 to 7 in 2025, the smallest number since Part D launched.
- The 2026 snapshot ties premium dynamics to the premium stabilization demonstration, noting that the federal monthly subsidy for participating PDPs fell from $15 in 2025 to $10 in 2026 while the allowed premium increase rose from $35 to $50.
- The same 2026 snapshot explains that Medicare reinsurance fell from 80% under older rules to 20% for brand spending and 40% for generic spending above the catastrophic threshold, with aggregate reinsurance projected at 18% of total Part D spending in 2026 versus 46% in 2024.
