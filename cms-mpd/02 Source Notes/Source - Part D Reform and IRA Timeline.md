# Summary

These imported sources describe the staged redesign of Medicare Part D from 2023 through 2026, especially the Inflation Reduction Act changes that reduce beneficiary cost sharing and change plan and manufacturer liability.

## Key Points

- In 2024, Part D beneficiaries no longer owed the 5 percent coinsurance in the catastrophic phase.
- In 2025, Part D moved to a $2,000 annual out-of-pocket cap and eliminated the old coverage gap phase.
- Imported reform material says the cap indexes upward after 2025; the PAN guide, last updated in October 2025, states a 2026 cap of $2,100.
- Other imported reforms include the $35 monthly insulin cap, no cost sharing for adult vaccines covered by Part D, broader Extra Help eligibility, and the Medicare Prescription Payment Plan that smooths payments over the year.
- The redesign also shifts more liability to plans and manufacturers above the catastrophic threshold and reduces Medicare reinsurance.
- CMS now treats the CY 2025 redesign instructions as the baseline and the CY 2026 redesign instructions as incremental updates, which is a useful way to organize policy-year comparisons across the vault.
- KFF's 2026 negotiation coverage analysis shows that all Part D enrollees now have coverage of all 10 selected drugs with negotiated prices available in 2026, including all dosage forms and strengths, and that coverage improved for several forms compared with 2025.
- The broader KFF negotiation facts note shows that negotiation is cumulative and multi-year: the first 10 Part D negotiated prices took effect on January 1, 2026, a second set of 15 Part D drugs takes effect in 2027, and a new 2026 selection of 15 Part B and Part D drugs is aimed at 2028 implementation.
- The Center for Medicare Advocacy and PAN deck adds two practical counseling points: beneficiary awareness of the new cap and payment plan is low, and premium stabilization limits the base premium trend but does not guarantee that every individual plan premium rises by 6 percent or less.

## Why It Matters

This is the main policy backbone for the rest of the vault. It affects beneficiary affordability, plan comparison, formulary behavior, counseling during open enrollment, and the interpretation of older Part D materials that still use the pre-2025 benefit structure.

## Useful Evidence

- The KFF brief lays out the pre-2025 four-phase structure and shows how the catastrophic phase, coverage gap, and cost-sharing rules changed in 2024 and 2025.
- The PAN guide explains the reforms from a beneficiary-facing angle and emphasizes that payment smoothing changes timing of payments, not the total owed.
- The 2024 CMA/PAN presentation explicitly frames 2025 around the $2,000 cap, the optional Medicare Prescription Payment Plan, inflation rebates, and premium stabilization.
- The newer negotiation pages are useful because they connect redesign to formulary access, selected-drug coverage mandates, and future GLP-1 coverage pressure rather than only to beneficiary cost sharing.
- Both imports tie plan review during open enrollment to current prescriptions, formularies, copays, and coinsurance rather than to broad marketing claims.
