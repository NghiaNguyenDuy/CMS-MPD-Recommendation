# Summary

This source set gives the vault a longitudinal oversight view of Part D before and after redesign. MedPAC's annual status reports track enrollment, spending, LIS, reinsurance, and incentives, while the DIR slide deck adds evidence on rebate variation and PBM or formulary structure.

## Key Points

- MedPAC's annual status reports show continued enrollment growth, with Part D covering more than 51 million beneficiaries in 2023, more than 54 million in 2024, and more than 55 million in 2025.
- Reported program totals also rose quickly: $117.3 billion in 2022 expenditures, $128.2 billion in 2023 payments, and $148.3 billion in 2024 payments.
- LIS remained a major financing channel throughout the period. MedPAC reported nearly 14 million LIS beneficiaries in 2023, $39.7 billion in LIS spending in 2022, $43.9 billion in LIS support in 2023, and $41.3 billion in LIS support in 2024.
- MedPAC repeatedly warned that cost-based reinsurance had become too dominant before redesign. By 2022 and 2023, more than 90 percent of Medicare's basic-benefit payments took the form of reinsurance rather than capitated direct subsidy.
- The 2025 redesign is important in MedPAC's framework because it restores more plan liability and more risk-based incentives, but the 2026 report says the transition also produced larger-than-expected bid and benefit-cost changes.
- The 2026 report ties recent spending acceleration partly to costly medicines such as GLP-1s, which makes MedPAC useful for connecting redesign, selected-drug pressure, and broader affordability debates.
- The DIR presentation shows that rebate levels varied widely across brand-name classes, from under 10 percent for antineoplastics to at least 50 percent for diabetic therapies, and that sponsor, PBM, contract, and formulary structure can materially affect those flows.

## Why It Matters

MedPAC's annual reports are one of the best recurring checkpoints for whether Part D is shifting in the direction policymakers expected. They connect beneficiary protections, plan incentives, spending growth, and market structure in one place.

## Useful Evidence

- The annual beneficiary counts are useful for tracking how Part D keeps growing even while PDP choice shrinks and sponsor structure changes.
- The reinsurance findings explain why pre-IRA Part D could weaken plan incentives to control costs and why redesign moved liability toward plans and manufacturers.
- The 2026 status report gives a concrete bridge between redesigned benefit structure and new spending pressure from high-cost drug classes.
- The DIR slides help explain why list prices, rebates, and beneficiary-facing prices can diverge sharply across classes and plan structures.
