# Summary

These NBER papers explain how Part D's original market design shaped plan incentives before the current redesign era. They focus on the interaction among risk adjustment, subsidy rules, formulary strategy, and the split between stand-alone PDPs and integrated MA-PD plans.

- [[01 Inbox/Web Imports/Strategic Formulary Design in Medicare Part D Plans|NBER paper on strategic formulary design]]

## Key Points

- McAdams and Schwarz argue that risk adjustment in a stand-alone drug benefit can create perverse incentives for plan coverage choices and pharmaceutical pricing rather than simply solving adverse selection.
- Their explanation is that stand-alone drug coverage is unusually vulnerable because patented drugs retain monopoly power and formularies let plans target very specific patient groups.
- The same paper argues that tighter regulation was likely necessary to prevent a race to the minimum acceptable formulary and suggests that a uniform-premium rule would trade some product diversity for lower budget uncertainty and less upward pressure on prices.
- Lavetti and Simon find that the fragmented structure of Part D changes formulary incentives. Integrated MA-PD plans can benefit from lower medical spending under Parts A and B, while stand-alone PDPs cannot internalize those cross-service savings.
- They also find evidence that integrated plans use formularies strategically to attract beneficiaries who are profitable under Parts A and B, which suggests that formulary generosity can reflect both efficiency and selection incentives at the same time.
- Decarolis, Polyakova, and Ryan model the subsidy system and conclude that consumers buy too few and too socially costly PDP plans, while insurers price close to marginal cost.
- Their welfare analysis says the current PDP subsidy design performs relatively close to an optimal in-kind subsidy, but remains far from a first-best solution and is highly sensitive to the opportunity cost of public spending.
- The same paper highlights that LIS assignment rules and the way MA-PD and PDP bids feed into subsidy calculations materially shape premiums, plan incentives, and welfare outcomes.

## Why It Matters

These papers give the vault a cleaner explanation for why Part D plan behavior can look strange from the beneficiary side. Formularies, premiums, and access rules are not just administrative details; they are responses to the program's incentive structure.

## Useful Evidence

- The McAdams and Schwarz paper is useful for explaining why drug-level risk adjustment and drug-level targeting can behave differently from standard health-insurance intuition.
- The Lavetti and Simon paper is one of the clearest explanations of why MA-PD and stand-alone PDP formularies may diverge even when they cover the same clinical area.
- The Decarolis, Polyakova, and Ryan paper connects insurer bidding, LIS rules, and government subsidy design to welfare results instead of treating premiums as purely a demand-side outcome.
