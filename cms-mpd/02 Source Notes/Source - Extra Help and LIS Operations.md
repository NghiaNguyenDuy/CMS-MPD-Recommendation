# Summary

This source set explains how the Part D Extra Help program works operationally, who is deemed eligible, who applies through SSA, and how the 2024 IRA change broadened access to full subsidy benefits.

## Raw Imports

- [[01 Inbox/Web Imports/SSA - POMS HI 03001.005 - Medicare Part D Extra Help (Low-Income Subsidy or LIS)|SSA POMS HI 03001.005]]
- [[01 Inbox/Web Imports/Everything you need to know about Medicare reforms|PAN Medicare reforms guide]]

## Key Points

- Some beneficiaries are deemed eligible and should not apply separately for Extra Help: SSI recipients, people with full Medicaid, and people in QMB, SLMB, or QI Medicare Savings Programs.
- SSA has primary responsibility for taking most Extra Help applications and making subsidy determinations; CMS handles deeming and enrollment support.
- For coverage start dates on or after January 1, 2024, the imported SSA POMS says IRA expanded full subsidy eligibility to people under 150 percent of the federal poverty level.
- A beneficiary can receive a full benchmark subsidy but still owe premium dollars if they choose a plan priced above the benchmark.
- CMS can auto-enroll or facilitate enrollment for some eligible beneficiaries who are not yet in a PDP or MA-PD.

## Why It Matters

Extra Help changes what a beneficiary actually pays and changes how counseling should work. It also prevents incorrect advice, such as telling deemed beneficiaries to complete unnecessary applications.

## Useful Evidence

- The SSA POMS explicitly distinguishes deemed eligibility from SSA application processing.
- The imported materials make clear that the 2024 reform removed the older partial-subsidy structure for the newly expanded income band and shifted those beneficiaries into full subsidy treatment.
- The POMS notes that SHIP and 1-800-MEDICARE are the referral points for plan selection questions, even when SSA handles subsidy determinations.
