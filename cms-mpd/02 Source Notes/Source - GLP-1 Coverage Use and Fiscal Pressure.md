# Summary

These sources show why GLP-1s are one of the biggest pressure points in current Part D policy. Use and spending are rising quickly, coverage of obesity indications is beginning to change through temporary models, and plans are responding with tighter utilization management and more coinsurance.

## Key Points

- KFF shows that GLP-1 use and spending in Medicare rose sharply between 2019 and 2024. Ozempic alone was used by 2 million Part D enrollees in 2024, up from fewer than 150,000 in 2019.
- Gross Part D spending on GLP-1s reached $27.5 billion in 2024, a five-fold increase from 2019, and KFF estimates that even after large rebates net spending could still be around $14 billion.
- Claims for GLP-1s increased from 4.8 million in 2019 to 21.8 million in 2024, with especially rapid growth for newer semaglutide and tirzepatide products.
- KFF's BALANCE explainer describes a two-step coverage expansion path: the Medicare GLP-1 Bridge from July 1, 2026 through December 31, 2026, followed by the BALANCE Model beginning in January 2027 if an 80 percent participation threshold is met.
- Under BALANCE, participating Part D plans must place model drugs on the same formulary tier, cover all medically accepted indications, and apply the same cost sharing to all indications.
- The same explainer notes that BALANCE participation is voluntary for plans and manufacturers, which creates risks of uneven access, plan switching, and premium effects if participation is limited or unstable.
- The open-access 2025 GLP-1 coverage study finds that broad diabetes-related coverage did not prevent access tightening. Prior authorization became nearly universal in 2025 and out-of-pocket costs rose sharply as plans shifted toward coinsurance.
- That increase was concentrated in stand-alone PDPs, while MA-PD plans generally maintained lower out-of-pocket costs, likely reflecting their ability to offset drug spending with medical savings or MA rebates.

## Why It Matters

GLP-1s sit at the intersection of obesity policy, drug affordability, plan liability, and premium pressure. They are a live example of how redesign-era incentives can produce broader coverage in some dimensions while tightening utilization management and beneficiary cost exposure in others.

## Useful Evidence

- The KFF spending trend note is useful for measuring the scale and speed of GLP-1 growth before obesity coverage expansion fully takes hold.
- The BALANCE explainer is useful because it translates CMS model design into practical risks around eligibility, participation, plan switching, and premiums.
- The open-access GLP-1 coverage study is useful because it shows how plans responded on the ground in 2025 through prior authorization and coinsurance.
