# Summary

These sources add an oversight layer to the Part D knowledge base. They show how redesign changes are working in practice, where affordability gains are already visible, and where drug-spending pressure is still growing fast.

## Key Points

- ASPE estimated that the IRA's LIS expansion would have benefited nearly 461,000 partial-LIS enrollees if it had applied in 2020.
- The same ASPE material says another 2.9 million Part D enrollees who were eligible but not enrolled in LIS would also have benefited, along with more than 661,000 beneficiaries without drug coverage who also appeared likely eligible.
- ASPE's 2024 out-of-pocket cap brief says more than half a million non-LIS enrollees reached the catastrophic cap by June 30, 2024, saving nearly $1 billion out of pocket, or about $1,802 each on average in the first half of the year.
- The OIG diabetes-drug brief says Medicare Part D spending on 10 selected diabetes drugs rose 364 percent from $7.7 billion in 2019 to $35.8 billion in 2023, while overall Part D enrollment rose only 12 percent over the same period.
- The OIG brief projects that spending on those 10 drugs could reach $102 billion by 2026, which makes the class a useful proxy for GLP-1 and selected-drug fiscal pressure.
- GAO reports that by December 2024 CMS had hired staff, issued guidance, negotiated the first 10 drug prices announced in August 2024 for 2026 use, and built inflation-rebate infrastructure with manufacturer invoicing planned for late 2025.
- GAO also found that CMS planned to obligate about $2.9 billion of the $3 billion negotiation appropriation and about $155 million of the $160 million inflation-rebate appropriation, mostly for program support, administration, rebate operations, and pharmacy-access infrastructure.

## Why It Matters

These materials turn policy changes into measurable implementation and affordability signals. They show that redesign is not only a statutory timeline but also an outreach problem for LIS, an operational challenge for CMS, and a fiscal-pressure problem in high-spend drug classes.

## Useful Evidence

- The ASPE LIS counts are useful because they separate currently enrolled beneficiaries from likely eligible people who are still missing the program.
- The ASPE cap brief gives an early real-world savings signal before the full 2025 capped structure took effect.
- The OIG diabetes-drug brief ties drug-class spending growth to program-wide risk, not just to one beneficiary subgroup.
- The GAO report is useful for tracking whether negotiation and inflation-rebate implementation are staying aligned with staffing, infrastructure, and appropriated funding plans.
