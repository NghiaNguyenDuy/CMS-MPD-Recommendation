# Summary

These OIG reports show that biosimilars are a major unrealized savings area in Part D. Coverage has improved, especially for Humira biosimilars, but plans still rarely use tiering or utilization management to actively steer beneficiaries toward lower-cost biosimilars.

## Key Points

- OIG's 2022 report says biologics cost Part D upward of $12 billion annually and that biosimilars remained underused relative to reference products even after biosimilar entry.
- In 2019, reference biologics were prescribed about five times more frequently than biosimilars in Part D.
- OIG estimated that Part D spending on biologics with available biosimilars could have been $84 million lower, or 18 percent, if all biosimilars had been used as frequently as the most used biosimilars, and beneficiary out-of-pocket spending could have been $1.8 million lower, or 12 percent.
- The 2022 report also found that not all formularies covered available biosimilars and that plans rarely used preferential tier placement or utilization management to encourage biosimilar use.
- OIG's 2025 Humira report shows that coverage improved substantially after Humira biosimilars entered the market. In 2025, 96 percent of PDPs and 88 percent of MA-PD plans covered at least one Humira biosimilar.
- Even so, some enrollees remained in plans that covered only Humira, and almost none of the formularies that covered both Humira and its biosimilars used preferential tier placement to encourage biosimilar use.
- The two reports together suggest that formulary inclusion is improving, but competitive pressure remains weaker than it could be because plans are often neutral between the reference product and biosimilars rather than actively favoring lower-cost options.

## Why It Matters

Biosimilars are one of the clearest tests of whether Part D plan design translates lower-price competition into actual beneficiary and program savings. These sources show that coverage progress alone does not guarantee strong substitution or strong savings.

## Useful Evidence

- The 2022 OIG report is useful for quantifying the spending and beneficiary savings left on the table when biosimilar uptake remains low.
- The 2025 Humira report is useful because it updates the story after a major blockbuster biosimilar launch rather than relying only on earlier small-market examples.
- Together they help distinguish three different issues: whether a biosimilar is on the formulary, whether plans actively favor it, and whether beneficiaries actually switch.
