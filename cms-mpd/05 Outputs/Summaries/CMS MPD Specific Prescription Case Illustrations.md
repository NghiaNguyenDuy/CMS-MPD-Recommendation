# Summary

This note turns the earlier case categories into prescription-level illustrations using drug names already present in the vault. The goal is not to estimate exact beneficiary liability for a named person, but to show how the same time-based cost logic applies to concrete prescriptions.

> [!info]
> Unless noted otherwise, these cases assume recurring `30-day` fills because the repository's runtime logic models fills across the year using normalized day-supply schedules.

> [!warning]
> These are counseling and research examples, not clinical prescribing guidance.

## Practical Reading Notes

- Cases `2`, `3`, `6`, and `11` are insulin-centered and should be read with [[Topic - Insulin Affordability in Part D]].
- Cases `4`, `5`, and `7` are strongest for explaining why fill ordering and policy year matter.
- Cases `8`, `9`, and `10` show that formulary inclusion does not automatically mean low OOP or easy real-world access.
