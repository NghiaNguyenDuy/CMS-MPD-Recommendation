# Summary

This note captures the practical enrollment and plan-selection workflow with `CY2025` as the anchor year, then compares how the same beneficiary decision logic differs in `CY2024` and `CY2026`.

## Why CY2025 Is The Anchor Year

- `CY2025` is the first full year of the redesigned standard Part D benefit.
- The standard structure moved to three phases: annual deductible, initial coverage, and catastrophic.
- The annual out-of-pocket threshold dropped to `$2,000`.
- The deductible rose to `$590`.
- The old coverage gap structure ended.
- Covered Part D drugs moved to `$0` beneficiary cost sharing in catastrophic.

## Real-Life CY2025 Enrollment Workflow

1. Confirm that the beneficiary can enroll, switch, or disenroll for `CY2025`.
2. Decide the coverage lane before shopping:
   `Original Medicare + standalone PDP` or `MA-PD`.
3. Build the beneficiary file:
   ZIP code, current coverage, Medicare effective dates, subsidy status, exact drug list, and actual pharmacy.
4. Screen for subsidy and special population status:
   Extra Help, Medicaid, employer coverage, retiree coverage, VA, TRICARE, or dual status.
5. Enter the exact drug and pharmacy data into Plan Finder.
6. Remove plans that are unsafe or incompatible:
   non-formulary essential drug, unusable pharmacy network, or unrealistic utilization management.
7. Compare the surviving plans on total annual fit:
   premium, deductible, actual cost sharing, formulary design, and pharmacy pricing.
8. Check monthly affordability separately from annual total.
9. Consider the Medicare Prescription Payment Plan if cash-flow is the main problem.
10. Enroll through Medicare, the plan, or 1-800-MEDICARE and save proof.

## CY2024 vs CY2025 vs CY2026

| Item | CY2024 | CY2025 | CY2026 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Standard deductible | `$545` | `$590` | `$615` |
| Annual Part D OOP threshold | `$8,000` | `$2,000` | `$2,100` |
| Coverage gap | Still part of benefit logic | Eliminated | Eliminated |
| Catastrophic beneficiary cost sharing | `$0` started in 2024 | `$0` continues | `$0` continues |
| Benefit structure | Older structure with IRA improvements layered on top | First full redesign year | Redesign continues with updated parameters |
| Insulin and adult vaccine protections | In effect | Continue | Continue |
| Medicare Prescription Payment Plan | Not available | Available | Available |
| Negotiation-era formulary effects | Not yet effective | Transition year for bids and sponsor behavior | More visible as selected-drug and negotiated-price policies begin to matter |

## Best-Plan Criteria By Beneficiary Type

### Low Utilizer

Profile:
- mostly generics
- limited expected annual drug spending
- usually does not approach the `CY2025` `$2,000` OOP threshold

Best-plan criteria in `CY2025`:
- lowest total annual cost at the actual preferred pharmacy
- routine formulary completeness
- deductible and early-year affordability
- simple administration

Interpretation:
The redesign matters less financially for this case because the beneficiary is unlikely to reach the cap. The plan decision is still mainly about premium, pharmacy fit, and generic pricing.

### Insulin User

Profile:
- one or more covered insulin products
- several additional chronic medications
- more sensitive to monthly cash flow

Best-plan criteria in `CY2025`:
- exact insulin coverage and stable access
- total annual cost for the entire regimen, not just insulin
- manageable monthly cash flow
- pharmacy and supply fit

Interpretation:
`CY2025` matters because insulin protections continue and the rest of the drug benefit becomes much more protective. If the person also has expensive non-insulin drugs, the new `$2,000` threshold materially changes the decision.

### High-Cost Specialty User

Profile:
- specialty oncology, rheumatology, neurology, transplant, or similar therapy
- prior auth, step therapy, or specialty pharmacy risk
- therapy interruption is clinically dangerous

Best-plan criteria in `CY2025`:
- exact specialty-drug access
- low utilization-management friction
- reliable specialty-pharmacy access
- lowest total annual cost among the safe options

Interpretation:
This is the case where `CY2025` changes the choice most. The problem is no longer mainly surviving a long path toward an `$8,000` OOP threshold as in `CY2024`; it becomes finding the safest-access plan and then minimizing cost under the new `$2,000` threshold.

## Decision Logic By Year

### CY2024

- Focus on surviving the older Part D structure with new IRA protections layered on top.
- For high spenders, when and how the beneficiary reaches catastrophic still drives a large part of the decision.
- Insulin and vaccine reforms help, but the overall benefit remains much harsher than `CY2025+`.

### CY2025

- Focus on redesigned-benefit plan fit.
- Total annual risk is much lower for heavy users, so formulary quality, pharmacy pricing, and utilization management matter more.
- Annual re-shopping becomes more important because plans are bidding into a new liability structure.

### CY2026

- Use the `CY2025` logic, then re-check all plans because deductible and OOP thresholds increase.
- Watch more closely for formulary substitutions, selected-drug effects, and negotiation-era pricing behavior.
- Do not assume last year's winning plan is still optimal.

## Practical Bottom Line

- `Low utilizer`: choose the cheapest clean plan.
- `Insulin user`: choose the plan that handles insulin and the full chronic regimen best, with attention to monthly affordability.
- `High-cost specialty user`: choose the safest-access plan first, then the cheapest among the safe plans.
