# Definition

Extra Help, also called the Part D Low-Income Subsidy, is the federal assistance program that lowers Part D premiums, deductibles, and copayments for eligible beneficiaries with limited income and resources.

## Core Details

- Some people are deemed eligible automatically through SSI, full Medicaid, or certain Medicare Savings Programs.
- Most non-deemed applications are handled through SSA, while CMS handles deeming and enrollment support.
- The imported SSA POMS says that for coverage start dates on or after January 1, 2024, the IRA expanded full subsidy treatment to people under 150 percent of the federal poverty level.
- ASPE estimated that nearly 461,000 partial-LIS enrollees would have benefited if the expanded full subsidy had applied in 2020.
- ASPE also identified 2.9 million Part D enrollees who appeared eligible but were not enrolled in LIS, plus more than 661,000 beneficiaries without drug coverage who also appeared likely eligible.
- A full subsidy does not always mean a zero premium if the beneficiary selects a plan priced above the benchmark.
- Extra Help interacts directly with plan selection, facilitated enrollment, and counseling workflows.

## Why It Matters

LIS status can change the beneficiary's real out-of-pocket exposure more than any single formulary rule. It is also a common source of confusion because application rules, deemed status, and benchmark premiums are handled by different entities, and the newer ASPE estimates show that under-enrollment remains a material policy problem.

## Examples

- A Medicaid beneficiary with Part A or B is generally deemed eligible and should not complete a separate Extra Help application.
- A beneficiary with income below 150 percent of the federal poverty level may qualify for full subsidy support under the post-2024 framework but can still owe premium differences for an above-benchmark plan.
