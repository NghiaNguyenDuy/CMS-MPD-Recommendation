# Definition

GLP-1 coverage and spending pressure describe how fast-growing use of glucagon-like peptide-1 drugs affects Part D spending, beneficiary access, formulary rules, and model-based coverage expansion for obesity.

## Core Details

- GLP-1 use and spending in Medicare grew rapidly from 2019 to 2024, especially for semaglutide and tirzepatide products.
- Coverage for diabetes indications has generally been broad, but access for obesity indications remains limited and is being expanded only through temporary model authority rather than a permanent statutory change.
- In 2025, plans tightened management of GLP-1s by making prior authorization nearly universal and increasing reliance on coinsurance, especially in stand-alone PDPs.
- CMS is using the Medicare GLP-1 Bridge in the second half of 2026 and the BALANCE Model in 2027 and later, if participation thresholds are met, to test broader obesity-related access.
- This topic links spending growth, beneficiary out-of-pocket burden, premium pressure, and plan participation risk in one drug class.

## Why It Matters

GLP-1s are one of the clearest examples of how a clinically valuable drug class can create simultaneous pressure for broader coverage and tighter plan management. They also show how temporary models can make beneficiary access contingent on plan participation and future bidding behavior.

## Examples

- A beneficiary who already uses a GLP-1 for diabetes may face higher coinsurance and prior authorization even while broader model-based obesity coverage is being discussed.
- A beneficiary who gains obesity coverage through the Medicare GLP-1 Bridge in late 2026 could still face disruption in 2027 if their Part D plan does not participate in BALANCE.
