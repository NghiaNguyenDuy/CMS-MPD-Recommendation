# Definition

Dual-eligible drug access and protections describe how Medicare Part D serves beneficiaries who are enrolled in both Medicare and Medicaid, especially through formulary coverage rules, auto-assignment processes, counseling support, and transition protections.

## Core Details

- Dual-eligible beneficiaries are high-need enrollees who generally receive prescription drug coverage through Medicare Part D rather than Medicaid drug coverage.
- OIG's annual monitoring shows that most Part D formularies have covered almost all drugs commonly used by dual-eligible enrollees since 2011.
- Broad formulary inclusion does not eliminate access risk because dual-eligible beneficiaries can still face transition problems, utilization management, or confusion during plan assignment and plan changes.
- Historical evidence from the 2006 transition to Part D suggests that removing Medicaid drug caps improved access to some medications and may have narrowed some racial disparities for dual enrollees with cancer.
- This topic overlaps with Extra Help, long-term-care transitions, and SHIP counseling because those are the main operational protections that keep coverage workable for beneficiaries.

## Why It Matters

Dual-eligible beneficiaries are among the least able to absorb interruptions in medication access. This topic matters because a technically compliant formulary can still produce access problems if assignment, transition-fill, counseling, or exception workflows fail.

## Examples

- A dual-eligible beneficiary may be auto-assigned to a benchmark plan with broad formulary coverage but still need counseling to resolve a utilization-management issue for a specific drug.
- A beneficiary transitioning from Medicaid drug coverage to Part D can benefit when federal rules remove state-level prescription caps that previously constrained access.
