# Definition

Biosimilar coverage and savings pressure describe how Medicare Part D turns biosimilar entry into actual formulary access, beneficiary use, and spending reductions.

## Core Details

- Biosimilars can lower spending only if plans cover them and create real incentives to use them instead of higher-cost reference biologics.
- OIG reports show that biosimilar inclusion on formularies has improved, especially after Humira biosimilars entered the market.
- Broad inclusion still does not guarantee strong substitution because plans often place Humira and its biosimilars on the same tier and apply similar utilization-management rules.
- Earlier OIG estimates show that low biosimilar uptake left measurable Part D and beneficiary savings unrealized even before the Humira wave.
- This topic overlaps with formulary design, rebate incentives, beneficiary cost sharing, and redesign-era pressure on high-cost drug classes.

## Why It Matters

Part D can miss a large share of potential biologic savings if biosimilars are technically covered but not operationally favored. This is one of the clearest cases where formulary design, not just drug approval, determines whether competition reaches beneficiaries.

## Examples

- A plan may list both Humira and a Humira biosimilar, but if both sit on the same tier with the same restrictions, the formulary is not strongly steering use toward the lower-cost option.
- Program spending can remain higher than necessary when biosimilars are available but uptake stays far below reference-product use.
