# Definition

Insulin affordability in Part D is the study of how benefit design, formulary placement, and policy protections shape the out-of-pocket costs Medicare beneficiaries face for insulin.

## Core Details

- Historical evidence in the imports shows rapid growth in insulin out-of-pocket spending before broader cost protections were added.
- Newer KFF evidence shows that insulin out-of-pocket spending in Part D exceeded $1 billion by 2020 and that 3.3 million beneficiaries used insulin that year.
- Beneficiary costs depended heavily on whether insulin was placed on flat-copay tiers or coinsurance tiers and whether the beneficiary reached the coverage gap.
- The Senior Savings Model was an early plan-based attempt to offer stable $35 monthly insulin costs in participating enhanced plans.
- The universal IRA insulin cap differs from the older model because it applies to all covered insulin products in all Part D plans and removes deductibles for Part D insulin.
- Later Part D reforms extended stronger insulin cost protection more broadly across the program.
- Insulin is also a strong example of how list-price growth and formulary strategy can work against adherence.

## Why It Matters

Insulin shows how a clinically essential drug can become unaffordable even when it is widely covered. It is one of the clearest case studies for linking policy, pricing, formulary design, and beneficiary behavior.

## Examples

- A beneficiary may pay a manageable copay for insulin in initial coverage and then see costs spike when a percentage-based phase or tier applies.
- A beneficiary shopping during annual enrollment must check whether the specific insulin product is covered and how it is tiered, not just whether a plan "covers insulin."
