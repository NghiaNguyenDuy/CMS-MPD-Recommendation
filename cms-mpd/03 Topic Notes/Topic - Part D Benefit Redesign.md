# Definition

The Medicare Part D benefit redesign is the multi-year shift from the older four-phase cost-sharing structure toward stronger beneficiary protections, lower catastrophic out-of-pocket exposure, and different plan and manufacturer liability.

## Core Details

- The older standard benefit had deductible, initial coverage, coverage gap, and catastrophic phases with changing beneficiary cost sharing.
- In 2024, beneficiary 5 percent catastrophic coinsurance ended.
- In 2025, the old coverage gap phase was removed and annual beneficiary out-of-pocket liability moved to a capped structure.
- The imported reform material says the cap was $2,000 in 2025 and indexed upward after that.
- ASPE's 2024 monitoring brief shows that more than half a million non-LIS enrollees hit the catastrophic cap by June 30, 2024, saving nearly $1 billion in the first half of the year and giving an early uptake signal before the full 2025 cap year.
- Related redesign elements include payment smoothing, insulin caps, vaccine no-cost-sharing, and broader Extra Help eligibility.
- Sponsor-facing guidance shows the redesign also changed annual bid assumptions, plan and manufacturer liability, and enrollment-support workflows rather than only beneficiary cost sharing.
- MedPAC's annual status reports explain why redesign was needed in the first place: before the IRA changes, cost-based reinsurance had grown to more than 90 percent of Medicare's basic-benefit payments, weakening plan incentives to manage drug spending.
- Earlier NBER market-design work in the vault also shows that pre-redesign Part D embedded strategic incentives through subsidy rules, risk adjustment, LIS assignment, and the split between stand-alone PDPs and integrated MA-PD plans.
- CMS treats CY 2025 redesign instructions as the baseline and CY 2026 redesign instructions as targeted updates, which is useful for sorting durable versus year-specific rules.
- Negotiated prices for the first 10 selected Part D drugs took effect on January 1, 2026, which means redesign now includes selected-drug coverage mandates and negotiation-era formulary access, not only the out-of-pocket cap.
- Pre-2026 coverage studies in the vault show that many of the selected drugs were already broadly covered before negotiated prices took effect, but actual access could still vary through tiering and utilization management.
- Biosimilar oversight material adds a parallel lesson: even when lower-cost competitors are on formularies, plans may not strongly promote them unless tiering and utilization-management incentives point in that direction.
- GLP-1 model documents and coverage studies show a similar redesign-era tension: CMS is expanding access through temporary models while plans simultaneously respond to high spending by increasing prior authorization and coinsurance.
- The Medicare Prescription Payment Plan is part of the redesign operationally as well as financially because plans, pharmacies, and CMS all have to communicate and administer it.

## Why It Matters

This concept is the anchor for interpreting almost every Part D source in the vault. Older evidence often describes pre-2025 rules, while current counseling and plan comparison require the redesigned framework.

## Examples

- A high-cost oncology drug user who once paid catastrophic coinsurance now reaches a capped point and stops paying additional covered Part D cost sharing.
- A beneficiary comparing plans in annual enrollment needs to understand that payment smoothing changes monthly cash flow, not total liability.

## Open Questions

- Which notes should explicitly label pre-2025 versus post-2025 benefit rules?
- How much do newer plan incentives change formulary behavior after liability shifts to plans and manufacturers?
