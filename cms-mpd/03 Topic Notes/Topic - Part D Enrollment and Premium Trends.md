# Definition

Part D enrollment and premium trends describe how beneficiary enrollment, plan availability, benchmark-plan access, sponsor concentration, and premium or deductible patterns change over time across stand-alone PDPs and Medicare Advantage drug plans.

## Core Details

- Part D enrollment continues to grow, reaching 56.1 million beneficiaries by February 2026.
- MedPAC's annual status reports show the same growth trajectory at earlier checkpoints, with more than 51 million beneficiaries in 2023, more than 54 million in 2024, and more than 55 million in 2025.
- MA-PDs dominate the individual market, but employer group strategy can materially change the overall MA-PD versus PDP split from year to year.
- Stand-alone PDP choice is shrinking, with fewer sponsoring firms and fewer plan options per beneficiary than in earlier years.
- LIS benchmark-plan availability is tightening, which makes reassignment risk and premium exposure more important for low-income beneficiaries.
- Premium and deductible patterns differ sharply across MA-PDs and PDPs, so beneficiary experience cannot be inferred from the base beneficiary premium alone.
- Redesign-era liability changes and the premium stabilization demonstration are shaping plan behavior, not just beneficiary cost sharing.

## Why It Matters

This topic connects market structure to counseling and plan comparison. Beneficiaries do not choose from an abstract policy design; they choose from whatever menu of plans, premiums, benchmark options, and formularies exists in their region that year.

## Examples

- A beneficiary in traditional Medicare may face fewer PDP choices in 2026 than in 2025 even while the Part D benefit is more protective overall.
- An LIS beneficiary can still encounter premium problems if enrolled in a non-benchmark PDP when benchmark options shrink.
